==============================================================================
Verdict
==============================================================================
  worst sigma over all 6,000 seed-pool trials: 4.216
  trials the dispersion rule would have failed at 5.0: 0, a false-alarm rate of 0.0000% against a budget of 0.1%
  margin above the worst observed: 0.78 sigma
  the superseded 4.0 would have failed 5 of them, which is what makes it unfit: a seed sweep is a routine thing to run.

  The floor at 0.75 sits below the smallest share any seed produced on the real
  snapshot (0.825), and it rejects a set at 0.58 of its intended size, which the derived
  bound alone accepted.

  On the 1,940-row fixture pool the smallest is 0.505, because 628 groups
  scatter far more than 29,564, so the floor would fire there on 230 of 2,000 seeds. The floor is a
  statement about a pool of the snapshot's granularity, and tests/test_split.py sweeps that
  pool against a looser floor, naming this measurement, rather than pretending otherwise.
