==============================================================================
Verdict
==============================================================================
  worst sigma over all 6,000 seed-pool trials: 4.249
  trials the dispersion rule would have failed at 5.0: 0, a false-alarm rate of 0.0000% against a budget of 0.1%
  margin above the worst observed: 0.75 sigma
  the superseded 4.0 would have failed 3 of them, which is what makes it unfit: a seed sweep is a routine thing to run.

  The floor at 0.75 sits below the smallest share any seed produced on the real
  snapshot (0.820), and it rejects a set at 0.58 of its intended size, which the derived
  bound alone accepted.

  On the 1,862-row fixture pool the smallest is 0.478, because 608 groups scatter far
  more than 28,435, so the floor would fire there on 243 of 2,000 seeds. The floor is a
  statement about a pool of the snapshot's granularity, and tests/test_split.py sweeps that
  pool against a looser floor, naming this measurement, rather than pretending otherwise.
